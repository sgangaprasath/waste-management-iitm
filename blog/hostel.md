### Different Categories of Waste Generated

#### Stationery Wastes:

1. **Paper Based** – Used notebooks or registers, bundles of textbooks, loose papers, empty cardboard boxes (from deliveries and snacks), art and craft papers → *Highly Recyclable/Biodegradable Dry Wastes*  
2. **Plastic Based** – Files/folders (often made of PVC or other mixed plastic), broken scales or rulers, pen/pencil components, parcel wrappers, tape dispensers, empty marker/highlighter pens → *Non-Biodegradable Dry Wastes*  
3. **Metal Based** – Stapler pins, paper clips, sharpener blades, compass components (maybe) → *Recyclable but Non-Biodegradable Dry Wastes*  
4. **Other Wastes** – Erasers, glues or adhesives, paint brushes → *Non-Biodegradable Dry Wastes*

---

#### Food Related Waste:

1. **Wet food wrappers** – *Non-Recyclable Waste* (not exactly biodegradable, just wet—common confusion)  
2. **Plastic food containers** (e.g., from takeaways), **plastic pouches** (e.g., milk pouches), **plastic wrappers** from snack or noodle packets → *Dry Waste but Recyclable only if clean or single-layered or rinsed*  
3. **Food scraps, fruit peels, leftover cooked food** (from mess, personal cooking, or takeaways), **tea bags**, **coffee grounds** → *Wet/Biodegradable Wastes*  
4. **Multi-layered food packets** (e.g., milk packets, biscuit packets), **chocolate wrappers**, **chip wrappers**, **expired food packets** (especially at semester ends), **Styrofoam cups/plates** → *Non-Recyclable Wastes*

---

#### Other Non-Recyclable Wastes

1. Sand/dust from rooms (when people clean rooms with a dustpan), hair, old window nets, empty plastic bottles, oil boxes  
2. Old discarded clothes/textiles (e.g., used handkerchiefs, doormats, curtains, etc.) (if not donated), worn-out footwear, old mattresses/furniture (especially at semester end) → *Alternative disposal or reuse methods to be explored*  
3. Broken items – Broken crockery (e.g., coffee mugs), broken glass (if not collected separately)  
4. Medical Waste (Small Scale) – Used bandages  
5. Contaminated Items – Heavily soiled food packaging, oily paper, used sanitary napkins, used cotton balls/buds, contaminated tissues

### Disposal Guidelines

| Waste Type | Disposal Guidelines |
|-------------|---------------------|
| **Dry Recyclable Waste** | Dispose of in blue bins. Rinse containers before disposal to avoid contamination. Specify the level of rinsing required (e.g., just water or with soap). Provide guidance for bulky items like big cardboard boxes—flatten them and place next to the bins or in a designated area. Include instructions for plastic caps (whether to keep them on or remove them). |
| **Wet/Biodegradable Waste** | Dispose of in green bins. Avoid disposing of liquids directly into bins without lining. Emphasize draining all liquids before disposal. Use bin liners for wet waste to prevent leakage and odor. |
| **Non-Recyclable Waste** | Dispose of in red bins. Ensure proper wrapping of heavily contaminated waste to avoid odor. Mandate wrapping all sanitary napkins in proper opaque bags. Provide clear instructions for broken glass (e.g., wrap in newspaper, label “broken glass” for safety). |

### Best Practices

1. Provide separate bins for the collection and disposal of **hazardous waste** and **e-waste**, which are currently lacking.  
2. Encourage hostel residents to minimize single-use plastics by using **steel or reusable containers**.  
3. For vacating students leaving behind mattresses or plastic equipment, encourage **reuse** through donation to students or staff in need (e.g., during internships or events).  
4. Conduct **regular waste audits** in hostels to track segregation compliance and waste generation rates.  
5. Review and optimize **number and placement of bins** on each floor and in common areas; collect feedback from residents on adequacy and maintenance.  
6. Monitor **waste segregation adherence** during hostel events or gatherings.
