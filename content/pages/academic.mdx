      ## Different Categories of Waste Generated


      ### Stationery and Academic Waste:


      **Paper Based**


      * Used worksheets, exam scripts, printed notes, assignment sheets, old
      registers, cardboard packaging (from book shipments or courier deliveries)
      \
        → Highly Recyclable/Biodegradable Dry Wastes

      **Plastic Based**


      * Laminated notices, plastic folders, ID pouches, plastic packaging for
      stationery, spiral-bound books with plastic covers\
        → Non-Biodegradable Dry Wastes

      **Metal Based**


      * Rusted paper clips, compass and divider components, staples\
        → Recyclable but non-biodegradable dry Wastes

      **Other Wastes**


      * Used whiteboard markers, adhesives, correction pens\
        → Non Non-biodegradable dry Wastes

      Misc: Wooden boxes that ship things


      ### Laboratory Waste:


      **Chemical Waste**


      * Reaction residues: Organic solvents like Acetone, Benzene, Toluene,
      Methanol\
        → Hazardous Waste
      * Salts, Acids, Buffers, Base solutions, Reaction media\
        → Hazardous Waste – Requires Neutralization

      **Contaminated Waste**


      * Used Filter Papers, Aluminum Foils, Tissues\
        → Potentially Hazardous if contaminated
      * Plastic Gloves, Microtips, Eppendorf tubes, Pipette tips\
        → Biohazardous or Chemical Waste depending on use

      **Microbiological Waste**


      * Petri dishes with mould/fungi cultures, agar plates, bacterial cultures\
        → Biohazardous Waste
      * Staining Dyes like Crystal Violet, Safranin\
        → Hazardous Waste

      ### E-Waste and Miscellaneous Waste:


      **E-Waste**


      * Dead batteries, broken instruments (multimeters, wires, heating
      mantles), old electronics\
        → E-Waste – Requires Separate Collection
      * Old discarded keyboards, mice, calculators, chargers\
        → E-Waste

      **Glassware**


      * Cracked test tubes, beakers, measuring cylinders\
        → Broken Glass – Non Recyclable, wrap and label

      **Occasional Discards**


      * Old lab coats, damaged lab stools, faulty equipment\
        → Non Non-recyclable wastes or Reusable with Repair

      ### Event/Conference Related Waste:


      **Food Waste**


      * Disposable cups, plates, cutlery from catered events\
        → Wet Waste + Non-Recyclable Plastics

      **Print Material**


      * Event Pamphlets, Printed Agendas, plastic banners\
        → Dry Waste, Recyclable if clean

      **Decorative Waste**


      * Thermocol, Balloons, Ribbons\
        → Non-Recyclable Wastes

      ### Waste Collection Points


      * E-Waste collection near Community Centre

      * Dry & Wet Waste depositing centre near Shopping Centre

      ## Disposal Guidelines


      | **Waste Type**           | **Disposal
      Guidelines**                                                                                                                                                                                   
      |

      | ------------------------ |
      ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      |

      | Dry Recyclable Waste     | Dispose of in blue bins. Ensure the paper is
      clean and dry. Rinse plastic containers before disposal. Flatten boxes.
      Use signage for separating dry lab plastic (like clean microtip
      boxes).               |

      | Wet/Biodegradable Waste  | Dispose of in green bins. Food leftovers and
      biodegradable waste from events should be drained and placed in lined
      bins. No liquids
      directly.                                                              |

      | Non-Recyclable Waste     | Dispose of in red bins. Wrap broken glass or
      contaminated items in paper/newspaper. Label as "sharp" if needed. Soiled
      packaging and multilayered food wrappers go
      here.                                   |

      | Hazardous/Chemical Waste | Use yellow containers or dedicated chemical
      disposal tanks. Follow lab-wise collection protocols. Never dispose of
      down the sink without neutralization. Tie-up with certified disposal
      agencies required. |

      | Biohazardous Waste       | Use autoclavable biohazard bags/containers.
      For contaminated gloves, tissues, Petri dishes, and biological material.
      Should not mix with regular
      waste.                                                    |

      | E-Waste                  | Set up e-waste bins/collection drives.
      Collect old batteries, electronics, and wires. Coordinate with the
      sustainability office for certified
      recycling.                                                   |

      ## Best Practices


      • Ensure each lab has color-coded bins for chemical, glass, plastic, and
      biohazardous waste.


      • Conduct training workshops for safe chemical and biohazard disposal.


      • Maintain chemical logbooks and waste disposal records in labs.


      • Conduct monthly e-waste collection drives at the department level.


      • Encourage reuse of partially used equipment (e.g., marker pens, gloves
      during demonstrations).


      • Display waste disposal posters near lab exits and in classrooms.


      • Create a feedback mechanism for students and staff to report bin
      shortages or improper disposal.


      • Consider periodic waste audits in science and engineering departments.
