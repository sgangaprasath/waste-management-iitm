      ### **Waste Collection Points**

      * 21 Hostels ( Boys + Girls) - Hostel Rooms, Corridors, Lawns or hostel grounds used for gatherings, Washrooms, Garden area, Cycle Stand
      * Mess Halls/Buildings - Eg. Cauvery, Himalaya, Nilgiri, Ganga Mess, etc.
      * Recreational Areas - Includes specific sports courts, gym areas, common rooms, and TV rooms within or adjacent to hostels.
      * Inter Hostel Areas - The pathways and green spaces between the hostel blocks.

      ### **Different Categories of Waste Generated**

      #### **Stationary Wastes:**

      1. Paper Based - Used Notebooks or Registers, Bundle of Textbooks, Loose Papers, Empty Cardboard Boxes( From Deliveries and snacks), Art and Craft papers →Highly Recyclable/Biodegradable Dry Wastes
      2. Plastic Based - Files/Folders( Often made of PVC or other mixed plastic), Broken Scales or Rulers, Pen/Pencil Components, Parcel wrappers, Tape Dispensers, Empty Marker/Highlighter pens → Non-Biodegradable Dry Wastes
      3. Metal Based - Stapler Pins, Paper Clips, Sharpener Blade, Compass Components(maybe) → Recyclable but Non-Biodegradable Dry Wastes
      4. Other Wastes - Erasers, Glues or Adhesives, Paint Brushes → Non-Biodegradable Dry Wastes

      #### **Food Related Waste:**

      1. Wet food wrappers: Non-Recyclable Waste (not exactly biodegradable, just wet, so don’t confuse, some ppl do that)
      2. Plastic Food Containers( eg, from takeaways), plastic pouches( eg, milk pouch), Plastic wrappers from snack or noodles packets →Dry Waste but Recyclable only if clean or single layered or rinsed.
      3. Food scraps, Fruit Peels, Leftover Cooked Food( all kinds - from mess, personal cooking or takeaways), Tea Bags, Coffee ground → Wet/Biodegradable Wastes
      4. All types of multi-layered food packets (e.g., milk packets, biscuit packets), chocolate wrappers, Chip wrappers, Expired Food Packets( especially at semester ends), Styrofoam cups/plates → Non-recyclable wastes

      #### **Other Non-Recyclable Wastes**

      1. Sand/dust from rooms(when people clean rooms with a dustpan), Hairs, Old window nets, empty plastic bottles, oil boxes
      2. Old discarded clothes/textiles(eg, used handkerchiefs, doormats, curtains, etc.)(if not donated), worn-out footwear, old mattresses/furniture (especially at semester end) -> but some other way to deal with it?
      3. Broken Items: Broken crockery (eg, coffee mugs), broken glass (if not collected separately).
      4. Medical Waste( Small Scale): Used bandages
      5. Contaminated Items: Heavily soiled food packaging, oily paper, used sanitary napkins, used cotton balls/buds, contaminated tissues.

      ### Disposal Guidelines

      | Waste Type              | Disposal Guidelines |
      | ----------------------- | ------------------- |
      | Dry Recyclable Waste    | Dispose of in blue bins. Rinse containers before disposal to avoid contamination. Specify the level of rinsing required (e.g., just water, or soap). Guidelines on how and where to dispose of bulky items like big cardboard boxes. For example, flatten them and place them next to the bins or a designated area. Some instructions for the plastic caps, that is, to keep them on or to remove them. |
      | Wet/Biodegradable Waste | Dispose of in green bins. Avoid disposing of liquids directly into bins without lining. Emphasize draining all liquids before disposal. Guidance on using bin liners for wet waste to prevent leakage and odor. |
      | Non-Recyclable Waste    | Dispose of in red bins. Ensure proper wrapping of heavily contaminated waste to avoid odor. Mandate the proper wrapping of all sanitary napkins in proper opaque bags. Clear instructions for broken glass (e.g., wrap in newspaper, label "broken glass" for safety). |

      ### Best Practices

      1. There should be separate bins for the collection and disposal of hazardous wastes and e-waste, which are not there at present.
      2. Encourage hostel residents to minimise single-use plastics by using steel or reusable containers.
      3. The vacating students leaving behind the mattress or some plastic equipment opt for re-use during internship programs or events. Given to the students or staff in need.
      4. Regular waste audits in hostels to track segregation compliance and waste generation rates.
      5. Specific numbers and placement of bins on each floor, common areas. Taking feedback from the students accordingly - Are the bins sufficient? Are they well-maintained?
      6. Monitor waste segregation adherence during hostel events or gatherings.
